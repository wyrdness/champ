## Overview

Champ is the popular name for a purported large aquatic cryptid said to inhabit Lake Champlain, a long, deep freshwater lake straddling the border of Vermont, New York, and the Canadian province of Quebec. Reports describe a serpentine or long-necked creature commonly compared with the Loch Ness Monster. Iroquois tradition records giant serpents in the lake — the Mohawk Onyare'kowa — long before European contact, and 19th-century newspaper reports describe sightings such as those of 'Captain Crum' (1819) and Sheriff Nathan Mooney (1883). The most famous modern piece of evidence is the 1977 photograph by Sandra Mansi, taken on July 5, 1977 at a bay on the Vermont side of the lake; the photograph's negative was discarded, and analysts have noted that the bay is only ~14 feet deep, complicating biological interpretations. Both Vermont (1982) and the New York town of Port Henry have passed measures protecting Champ from harm. The creature is one of the most prominent North American lake-monster legends and an important regional cultural and tourism symbol; mainstream scientific consensus regards Champ as a folkloric and misidentification phenomenon rather than a confirmed animal.

- **First use**: Popularised in late-19th-century regional press; widespread by the 20th century.

## Theories & Explanations

### Scientific

**Relict population of unknown large aquatic animal** _(Cryptozoologists including Joseph Zarzynski)_

Hypothesis that Champ represents a small population of an unknown or relict large aquatic animal — variously suggested as a long-necked plesiosaur-like reptile or a giant eel — surviving in Lake Champlain.

**Misidentified known species** _(Joe Nickell (CSI), Benjamin Radford)_

Champ sightings are best explained by misidentified known animals: lake sturgeon (which can exceed 7 feet), garfish, otters in line, or floating logs and wave-trains.

### Skeptical

**Cultural / tourism-driven legend** _(Skeptical Inquirer, Joe Nickell)_

Champ's modern profile is closely tied to regional tourism; the legend has been actively promoted by local government and the Vermont Lake Monsters baseball team, which uses Champ as its mascot.
